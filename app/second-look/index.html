05 · The news

Jev, a model that answers software with decisions

Launched 15 September 2026. Typed questions in, typed answers out.

$40mseed, led by DCVC
70–500 msclaimed latency
$0.042per million input tokens
API onlyno weights, no browser version
Read the full brief and our view

The company

Founded by Diogo Almeida, a former OpenAI researcher who worked on the methods behind ChatGPT, with Erik Gafni and Sasha Sheng. A $40m seed led by DCVC, at a reported $200m valuation.

Sources: DCVC · Forbes · Dealroom

What Jev returns

  • Choice: one of up to 255 labelled options, with a probability for each.
  • Score: a place on an ordered scale of 2–10 levels, with the distribution.
  • Yes or no (Jev calls it "noul"): a probability from 0 to 1.

Training for calibration is the pitch: answers given 90 % should be right about 90 % of the time. The docs publish no calibration measurement.

Sources: launch post · docs

The claims, and whose tests they are

  • 70–500 ms end to end; 40–200× faster than frontier LLMs on "System One" tasks.
  • One workflow: 193.6× faster and 444.6× cheaper than a GPT-class model. TypeSafe's own team wrote the workflows and says the gains sit at the high end.
  • $0.042 per million input tokens; output is free.
  • Writing on LinkedIn, Alexandre Dos Santos notes that fine-tuned classifiers already in production run 5–10× faster than Jev, at almost no cost, on in-house servers.
Sources: launch post · MarkTechPost

How you would use it, and where the data goes

  • A hosted API (POST api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone) with Python and JavaScript SDKs. No weights, no self-hosting, no browser version.
  • The playground needs a sign-in (Google or an emailed code).
  • The docs name no EU region. TypeSafe says it does not train on customer data, offers a data processing agreement, and offers zero data retention to enterprise customers.
Sources: quickstart · legal
Our read

The real edge is that Jev needs no task-specific training, so a new decision could go live without labelled data. For insurers the calibrated probability matters more than the speed: it sets the straight-through threshold. Act above it, refer to a person below it.

This page shows the other half: typed decisions with a probability are no longer rare. Two free models of 13 MB and 23 MB do a useful part of it on a laptop, offline. What is still scarce is a probability you can act on without labels. The telling test is one claims-routing decision, scored against the classifier already in place, with the data processed in the EU.

What runs where

The page, the runtime, the Word reader and both models are served with the page and kept in this browser: the models in IndexedDB, the rest by a service worker, so a reload works with no connection. Decisions, anonymisation and variants are computed in this tab, on this device's processor.

What the counter can see

The request counter in the bar below reads this page's own performance timeline. The models run in the page itself, so their work is inside its view. Your browser's DevTools → Network shows the same.

Limits

  • English-language models; French and German text works less well.
  • Small models make mistakes: every result shows its probability.
  • Numbers under each widget were measured on 30 synthetic messages written for this page.
