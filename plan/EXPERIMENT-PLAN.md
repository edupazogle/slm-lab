# SLM Lab — experiment plan

2026-09-21 · supervisor session · companion to [`../prd/PRD.md`](../prd/PRD.md) and [`../prd/DECISIONS.md`](../prd/DECISIONS.md)

This plan turns the phase-1 critique ([`../critique/CRITIQUE.md`](../critique/CRITIQUE.md)) and the research
([`../research/`](../research/)) into experiments that can each **prove or kill** one idea. Every experiment names
the BizLoop requirement it serves, a dataset with a size, a metric, a pass bar written *before* the run, the
effort, and its status. An experiment without a pass bar is a demo, and is labelled as one.

## Rules every experiment follows

These come from what went wrong in phase 1 and in this session (DECISIONS D5).

1. **Datasets are generated by code that is not the training generator.** Templates, names and phrasings used for
   evaluation are disjoint from those used for training, checked by an assertion (as `experiments/v2/ft2_make_data.py`
   does for query overlap and label grounding). Every string label is a verbatim span of its input.
2. **Bilingual by construction.** Every set is 50 % French, 50 % English, and every metric is reported per language.
3. **n is big enough to see the effect you claim.** Report a 95 % bootstrap interval; a difference whose paired
   interval crosses zero is "no measured difference", not "same".
4. **Timings separate the phases** (load, prompt processing, decode), include a warm-up, repeat at least three times,
   read model files from ext4 (never from `/mnt/e` over 9p), and record the machine load at run time.
5. **Each pipeline stage checks the artifact of the previous one** and stops loudly when it is missing.
6. **Telemetry off**: `NEEDLE_TELEMETRY=0 DO_NOT_TRACK=1 HF_HUB_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` in every script.
7. **A result is a file.** Inputs, full outputs and the scorer's output are saved next to the script; the findings
   paper cites the file, never a number typed from memory.

## The experiments, in priority order

Priority = value to an insurer's AI-innovation team × how directly it answers a BizLoop requirement ÷ effort.
"BizLoop hook" cites the refactor PRDs in `docs/copilot-experience/` and `docs/refactor/mvp_bizloop_refactor/`.

### E1 — On-device anonymisation gate (highest value)

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §7: partner tools answer with "source named, residency noted" (CB Insights,
  Harvey); Implementation PRD v2 §5 residency rules: AXA data only reaches EU-resident deployments. A gate that
  pseudonymises text *on the device* before it goes to a partner tool outside the EU is the direct answer.
- **Hypothesis.** A regex + NER + small-model ensemble reaches entity-level recall ≥ 0.95 on French and English
  claim text, which is the level at which a human spot-check becomes practical.
- **Dataset — licence-checked.** 400 FR + 400 EN rows from **OpenPII 1.5M (CC-BY-4.0**, 1.64 M rows, 30 languages
  incl. French, with spans) + 100 hand-written French claim emails with NIR (13+2, key check), IBAN FR76, SIV plates
  (AB-123-CD), SIREN, dates of birth, two named persons, a garage name.
  **Do not use `ai4privacy/pii-masking-300k` or `400k`: they are academic-use only**, which rules them out for AXA
  work. This corrects an earlier version of this plan.
- **Systems — licence-checked.** (1) regex baseline (email, FR phone, NIR with key check, IBAN mod-97, plate, SIREN,
  dates); (2) Presidio 2.2.364 (note: the repo moved to `data-privacy-stack/presidio`) with `fr_core_news_lg` **plus
  custom French recognisers we must write — Presidio ships no `france` folder**, though French is in
  `PhoneRecognizer.DEFAULT_SUPPORTED_REGIONS`; (3) GLiNER2-PII (`fastino/gliner2-privacy-filter-PII-multi`,
  Apache-2.0); (4) `openai/privacy-filter`; (5) needle3 extraction with list-typed fields; (6) a 1–2 B Apache/MIT
  GGUF with JSON-schema output (the chat app's "Anonymise" skill); (7) the union of the best two.
  **Blocked by licence, do not test as candidates:** LFM2.5-Encoder-PII (LFM 1.0 forbids commercial use above
  $10 M revenue), Piiranha (CC-BY-NC-ND), Meddies (CC-BY-NC).
- **Reversibility.** Presidio `encrypt` / `decrypt` for the round trip; note `hash` salts randomly since 2.2.361, so
  it cannot be used for a stable mapping.
- **Metrics.** Per-type recall at boundary matching (recall is what matters), precision, document-level leak rate of
  direct identifiers, the round trip pseudonymise → send → re-identify (exact restoration rate), latency per page in
  the browser and on the CPU.
- **Pass bar (calibrated against published numbers).** Document-level leak rate of direct identifiers ≤ 1 % per
  language at precision ≥ 0.80; recall ≥ 0.98 on the regex-shaped types (NIR, IBAN, phone, email, plate) and ≥ 0.95
  on PERSON; 100 % placeholder round trip. Context for the bar: the best of five systems on the SPY benchmark
  reaches span F1 0.471 (`openai/privacy-filter` 0.373), so a *span-F1* bar near 0.95 would be unreachable — the
  bar is on leak rate, which is the risk that matters.
- **Kill rule.** If the regex baseline alone meets the leak-rate bar, ship the regex and drop the model.
- **Effort.** M (2–3 days). **Status.** Not started. Research: `../research/landscape/anonymisation-synthetic-docling-finetuning.md` §1.

### E2 — Confidence-gated claims triage (act / confirm / refuse)

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §2 N2 "only decisions reach the user, with a recommendation" and the decision
  card; the risk bands the chat app's "Triage a claim" skill displays.
- **Hypothesis.** A small router can auto-handle a useful share of simple claims at a low error rate if, and only
  if, its confidence is calibrated. Phase 1's evidence for this was an off-by-one misreading (CRITIQUE).
- **Dataset.** `triage_gold.jsonl`, 200 messages (100 EN / 100 FR): 80 complete claims, 50 incomplete, 30
  callbacks with relative time, 20 multi-action, 20 off-topic; 160 from Faker over 12 templates disjoint from the
  training generators, 40 hand-written hard cases (two persons, amount in words, negation, "avant-hier").
- **Systems.** needle3 base (the only needle with a calibrated confidence head), LFM2-1.2B-Tool, Qwen3.5-0.8B /
  2B with JSON schema, FunctionGemma-270M.
- **Metrics.** Routing accuracy, per-argument exact match, ECE (10 bins), AUROC of confidence vs correctness, and a
  risk-coverage table sweeping the act threshold t ∈ {0.5 … 0.99}.
- **Pass bar.** At some threshold, ≥ 40 % coverage with ≤ 2 % error among auto-acted messages, in both languages.
  Otherwise the triage skill stays advisory ("confirm" band only).
- **Effort.** M. **Status.** Not started. Note from forensics (DECISIONS D4): fine-tuning removes needle's confidence,
  so the router must be the *base* model.

### E3 — needle3 fine-tune v2 (does tuning move field accuracy?)

- **BizLoop hook.** Implementation PRD v2 §5 "small model first, escalate on rejection": a tuned specialist is the
  "small model" of the cascade.
- **Hypothesis.** A LoRA on 1,200 grounded, leakage-checked EN+FR rows raises field micro-F1 over the untuned model
  at the same 4-bit precision.
- **Design.** `experiments/v2/`: train 4 epochs, rank 32 (`ft2_pipeline.sh`); three conditions × two prompt modes:
  base 2-bit (as shipped), base 4-bit (zero-delta adapter, removes the precision confound), tuned 4-bit; 240 eval
  cases; field micro-F1 with bootstrap CIs and a paired bootstrap tuned − base4bit (`ft2_score.py`).
- **Pass bar.** Paired 95 % interval for tuned − base4bit field F1 entirely above +0.05, with no rise in false calls
  on off-topic input.
- **Status: PASSED, 2026-09-21** (240 cases). Fine-tuning alone, against the 4-bit untuned control so quantisation is
  held constant: field F1 **+0.176, 95 % CI [+0.141, +0.210]**, tool accuracy +0.096, exact +0.433, P(Δ ≤ 0) = 0.000;
  the false-call rate on off-topic input **falls from 0.281 to 0.000**. French improves most (field F1 0.58 → 0.91).
  Quantisation alone (the shipped 2-bit vs our 4-bit export) is worth +0.069, which is why phase 1's comparison —
  2-bit base against a 4-bit tuned model — could not attribute anything.
- **The cost side, measured.** Training was 9 min 37 s on the 4070 for 1,200 rows × 4 epochs, but our 4-bit export
  is **~2.7× slower and 1.8× larger** than the shipped 2-bit artefact (0.46 → 1.63 s per case natively). Accuracy was
  bought with latency and size; on a phone that trade may not be worth it.
- **Effort.** S. Files: `experiments/v2/ft2_score.txt`, `ft2_report.json`. Write-up: [`../findings/FINDINGS-v2.md`](../findings/FINDINGS-v2.md).

### E4 — Structured extraction bake-off

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §7 "Parse these PDFs into a table" (sandbox, docling) and the claim-fields
  skill; Implementation PRD v2 §5 bake-off discipline applied to the small tier.
- **Dataset.** `extract_gold.jsonl`, 120 claim texts with gold JSON (5-field record), 60 EN / 60 FR, 100 from Faker
  over 10 unseen paraphrase templates + 20 adversarial (two amounts, two dates, a third-party name, amount in words,
  a docling-style table).
- **Systems.** needle3, needle2, LFM2-350M-Extract, LFM2-1.2B-Extract, NuExtract-1.5-tiny, NuExtract-2.0-2B,
  Osmosis-Structure-0.6B, Qwen3.5-0.8B / 2B, FunctionGemma-270M — every GGUF model with grammar-constrained JSON so
  the comparison with needle's constrained decoder is fair.
- **Metrics.** Field micro-F1, exact-record match, hallucinated-field rate, per-language split, latency.
- **Pass bar.** A model ≤ 1.2 GB reaches field F1 ≥ 0.90 in both languages → it becomes the default of the
  claim-fields skill. **Effort.** M. **Status.** Not started; candidates in `../research/models/slm_models.json`.

### E5 — Throughput, measured properly (desktop CPU, GPU, browser, phone)

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §7 "anything over ten seconds says so with an estimate"; the cost line.
- **Design.** (a) llama.cpp native, `experiments/v2/bench_llamacpp.py`: model copied to ext4, page cache warmed,
  one warm-up, three repeats, prefill and decode reported separately, threads 4 / 8 on the 4-core VM, CPU and CUDA
  builds. (b) The same models in the browser through the app's `bench.html` (wllama, single- and multi-thread,
  WebGPU where available). (c) The Android APK on the operator's phone (Track A, WebView) and, later, the Track B
  native build.
- **Models.** SmolLM2-360M Q8_0, LFM2.5-350M, Qwen3.5-0.8B, LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct, Qwen3.5-2B, Llama-3.2-3B (Q4_K_M).
- **Pass bar.** No pass/fail: this produces the numbers `app/web/src/data/findings.json` displays. Each number is
  accepted only with its machine string and load recorded.
- **Effort.** S. **Status.** Functional runs verified; timed runs pending a quiet machine (the one attempted on
  2026-09-21 was stopped because the CPU was shared with a GPU fine-tune).

### E6 — French stress test

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2: French at pilot; AXA France entities.
- **Dataset.** 60 French items: accented perils (dégât des eaux, bris de glace, vol avec effraction, catastrophe
  naturelle), French dates ("le 1er août", "avant-hier"), amounts ("1 250,50 €", "mille deux cents euros"), phone
  formats, plates — plus the French halves of E1, E2, E4.
- **Metrics.** Field F1 FR − EN delta; exact reproduction of accented gold strings; token ratio FR/EN; latency ratio.
- **Pass bar.** FR within 5 points of EN for the models kept in the product; a model failing it is labelled
  "English only" in the model shelf. **Effort.** S (piggybacks on E1/E2/E4). **Status.** Not started.

### E7 — Document understanding: docling → small extractor

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §7 sandbox "Parse these PDFs into a table" (docling named).
- **Dataset.** 40 documents with gold key-values: 10 born-digital French claim forms with multi-row tables (3 of
  them 3 pages with a table across a page break); the same 10 rasterised at 150 dpi, rotated 2–5°, JPEG q40, noise
  and a stamp (forces OCR); 10 French forms from XFUND; 10 phone photos of a printed constat amiable with
  handwriting (taken by the operator, or marked NOT TESTED).
- **Pipelines.** docling 2.129.0 (installed here, with rapidocr 3.9.2; no EasyOCR or Tesseract) standard pipeline +
  **RapidOCR with `--ocr-lang iso:fr`** + TableFormer, then docling Markdown → the E4 winner, table kept vs
  flattened to prose. granite-docling VLM only as an A/B — **it does not list French**.
- **Three traps to avoid, all verified.** RapidOCR **defaults to Chinese** (`lang=["ch"]`) — set French explicitly;
  `auto` engine selection picks a different engine per machine, so pin it; and docling issue **#4255** reports
  TableFormer `accurate` **silently dropping all data rows**, a regression present in the version we have — so the
  run must assert a non-zero row count per table and fail loudly if it is zero (D5: an instrument that cannot fail
  is not an instrument).
- **Metrics.** Key-value accuracy per document class, table cell accuracy (TEDS), empty-table rate, cold and warm
  seconds per page. Yardstick: docling's own evaluation reports TableFormer TEDS 0.90 on FinTabNet but 0.69–0.80 on
  OmniDocBench, so expect the harder set to be much worse.
- **Pass bar.** Field accuracy ≥ 0.90 born-digital, ≥ 0.75 rasterised, **empty-table rate 0**, ≤ 8 s per page on the
  4070. **Effort.** M. **Status.** Not started.

### E8 — Synthetic claims for testing AI systems

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §2 N6 "governed quality" and the bake-off (Implementation PRD v2 §5: an
  evaluation set of 40 tasks + 20 skill runs) — synthetic claims are how the evaluation set is widened without AXA data.
- **Design.** 200 French motor / home claims per model under a JSON schema (policy-number pattern, ISO date within 24
  months, amount, line-of-business enum, narrative ≥ 200 characters), temperature 0.9, seeded with 20 peril ×
  circumstance × persona combinations; in the browser via the chat app's "Synthetic claims" skill and natively.
- **Grammar traps, verified.** In llama.cpp's JSON-schema → GBNF conversion, `minimum` / `maximum` are honoured for
  `integer` but **not for `number`**, so an amount field can come back unbounded; unsupported schema features are
  **skipped silently**; and the schema is **not injected into the prompt**, so the prompt must still describe the
  fields. Assert the business rules after generation rather than trusting the grammar.
- **Metrics.** Schema-valid rate; business-rule validity (date not in the future, amount within a peril range,
  narrative mentions the peril); diversity (distinct-2, self-BLEU, duplicate names / policy numbers); privacy leak
  measured as verbatim seed spans in the output (**not** nearest-neighbour distance — distance-to-closest-record is
  not a privacy metric); optionally Anonymeter, which CNIL has reviewed.
- **Pass bar.** ≥ 95 % business-rule valid, self-BLEU ≤ 0.35, ≤ 1 % duplicates, **zero verbatim seed spans longer
  than 8 tokens**, and a downstream gain of ≥ 3 F1 with a CI excluding zero when the synthetic set is added to
  training data. **Effort.** S. **Status.** Not started.

### E9 — Retrieval for document Q&A (embedders, on device and on the server)

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §7 knowledge tier ("answer with wiki citations", P1).
- **Dataset.** 30 French policy / general-conditions excerpts + 10 English (~400 chunks), 60 hand-written questions
  (40 FR / 20 EN) each mapped to its gold chunk.
- **Systems.** multilingual-e5-small, bge-m3, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, EmbeddingGemma-300M, granite-embedding-97M,
  needle3 embeddings; plus Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B. On the server: stock AnythingLLM configured by API (DECISIONS D1, D6).
- **Metrics.** recall@1 / @5, MRR per language, ms per chunk (CPU and wllama). **Pass bar.** recall@5 ≥ 0.9 in
  French for an embedder ≤ 350 MB → it becomes the in-browser RAG default. **Effort.** M. **Status.** Not started.

### E10 — In-browser needle3 (WebAssembly) vs native

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §8 trust scene "Where does my data go?" — a tool-calling specialist that runs
  in the page.
- **Design.** `app/web/src/lib/needle/` wraps needle3's own 0.75 MB WebAssembly engine; run the first 50 rows of the
  E2 set in Chromium and compare with the native Python engine.
- **Metrics.** Cold load seconds, per-call ms, agreement with native (must be 100 % or explained).
- **Pass bar.** 100 % agreement and ≤ 1 s per call on a laptop. **Effort.** S. **Status.** Wrapper written; its
  first headless self-test hung for 10 minutes on 2026-09-21 because the repo directory was renamed mid-run — re-run.

### E11 — Local watchdog classifier for the agent fleet (zero token cost) — **run first**

- **BizLoop hook.** The harness itself (`CLAUDE.md`, "the agent-failure lesson"): delegates that garble or sit idle
  unnoticed. A small model classifying terminal captures as working / garbled / idle / waiting-for-permission runs
  every 15 s at no token cost. Ranked first for value × feasibility in
  `../research/landscape/bizloop-applications.md` §4 — the data is already on disk (59 stream-json transcripts,
  ~258 MB; 200+ `tmux capture-pane` captures).
- **Metrics and pass bar.** Garble recall ≥ 0.95 overall and ≥ 0.90 on the Latin-script slice (where the current
  non-Latin-ratio heuristic scores about zero); ≤ 1 false alarm per 8 agent-hours; pane-state accuracy ≥ 0.90 with
  waiting-for-permission recall ≥ 0.95; ≤ 1 s CPU per poll.
- **Kill rule.** If a compression-ratio + regex baseline comes within 2 points, keep the baseline and drop the model.
- **Effort.** S–M. **Status.** Not started.

### E13 — Claim extraction for the review digest (demote-only) — **run first**

- **BizLoop hook.** Classifying what an agent report claims into did / verified / claimed-not-verified for the
  review digest (Experience PRD v2 §8, "learning made explicit"; the repo's own rule that a delegate's report is a
  claim).
- **Dataset.** 53 finished job reports + 22 brief reports already in the repo (~400 labelled claims).
- **Metrics and pass bar.** Claim recall ≥ 0.95, precision ≥ 0.80, and **zero promotions**: the schema has no
  "verified" value, because "verified" can only come from joining the event log. The model may demote a claim, never
  promote one. **Kill rule.** A regex cue list reaching recall ≥ 0.90.
- **Effort.** M. **Status.** Not started.

### E14 — A self-hosted small model as an ordinary `config/models.yaml` binding (half-day spike) — **run first**

- **BizLoop hook.** Implementation PRD v2 D2: "the harness is model-agnostic by contract … configuration, not code".
  Both E1 and E13 need a small model to be reachable in-tenant before they can ship, so this is the plumbing test.
- **Pass bar.** A local OpenAI-compatible endpoint serves a role binding end to end, with its cost line and model
  card, and no code change. Additionally: any server-side small model must beat the first-party nano-class models on
  the same task before it earns a model card — otherwise use the nano model.
- **Effort.** S. **Status.** Not started.

### E12 — WASM (Track A) vs native (Track B) on the same phone

- **BizLoop hook.** Experience PRD v2 §4 (reach everyone where they are) and the "offline field use" candidate.
- **Design.** The same two models (SmolLM2-360M, Qwen3.5-0.8B) in the Track A APK (Capacitor WebView + wllama) and
  in a Track B build (anythingllm-mobile fork, llama.rn), same prompts, same phone, battery and thermals logged.
- **Metrics.** TTFT, decode tok/s, peak RSS, battery % per 1,000 generated tokens, and throughput after 10 minutes
  of continuous use (an iPhone 16 Pro has been reported to lose nearly half its throughput within two iterations to
  thermal throttling — measure the same on Android).
- **Published yardstick for the native side.** LiquidAI reports LFM2.5-1.2B Q4_0 at 335 prefill / 70 decode tok/s on
  a Galaxy S25 Ultra, and Google reports Gemma 3 1B int4 at 133 decode tok/s on a MacBook M4 Max through WebGPU
  (`../research/landscape/slm-landscape-2026.md`). Our own in-browser measurement on this WSL machine — 29 prefill /
  12.3 decode tok/s for a 360 M model on 4 CPU threads with no WebGPU — is two orders of magnitude away from the
  first and is a floor, not a phone number.
- **Pass bar.** Decides D3: if Track A is within 2× of Track B, Track A remains the only Android app. **Effort.** M.
  **Status.** Track A debug APK built 2026-09-21; Track B not started.

## Order of work

The BizLoop mapping (`../research/landscape/bizloop-applications.md` §5) picks **E11, E1 and E13** as the three to
run first, with **E14** as the half-day spike both E1 and E13 depend on. The product experiments (E3, E5) finish
alongside them because the app is already built and the numbers are what it displays.

1. **Week 1:** E3 (finish scoring), E5 (quiet-machine timings → `findings.json`), E14 (spike), E11 dataset + baseline.
2. **Week 2:** E11 decided; E1 dataset + regex / Presidio / GLiNER baselines; E13 labelling.
3. **Week 3:** E1 small-model systems and the union; E13 decided; E2; E6 folded into both.
4. **Week 4:** E4, E8, E10; then E7, E9, E12 (needs the operator's phone).

Every experiment carries a **kill rule** — the cheaper baseline that, if it wins, ends the idea. An experiment
without one is a demo.

Each finished experiment updates `../findings/FINDINGS-v2.md` (result + file), `app/web/src/data/findings.json`
(numbers the app shows) and, if a pass bar is met, the PRD's model shelf or skill defaults.
